Space launches and debris is a growing problem

As the frequency of space launches continues to rise, the accumulation of artificial objects and orbital debris has become increasingly significant. This growing presence of debris poses substantial risks to operational satellites, spacecraft, and the International Space Station. Additionally, it interferes with astrophotography by obstructing clear views of the night sky.

It hurdles through space at lethal speed

Space debris can travel up to speeds of 18,000 mile per hours which even something the size of a nickel can cause billions of dollars of damage and cascading effects across our planet’s orbit.

And it is an existential threat to our species’ future

Donald Kessler coined a term called the Kessler effect in which the amount of debris encircling our planet becomes too numerous, entombing our species to the Earth’s surface and rendering any efforts to venture further out into space too dangerous.

Looking Ahead

As we accelerate the pursuit of space exploration, we must also recognize that with more space exploitation and exploration, more growing consequences of our actions beyond Earth. Launches are becoming more frequent, but so is the accumulation of debris: a yet invisible affect that threatens the very progress we celebrate.

Spcae exploration is not just about reaching new frontiers, but preserving them. Through global cooperation, sustainable technology, and responsible policy, we have the opportunity to protect our orbital environment while also exploring the outer world.

The question now is: can we continue to look outward without compromising our future?
